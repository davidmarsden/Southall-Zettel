Ealing Council has told Southall residents that it updated its equality assessment, consulted partners and considered local impacts when it moved its drug and alcohol treatment service, RISE, into Southall Town Hall. It has also said there is "no evidence" that the move is behind the problems residents are reporting.


In two Freedom of Information responses dated 16 October, the council has set out what records it holds to back those statements. 

On most of the questions, the answer is none.

## The equality assessment that wasn't updated


The council's RISE webpages say it "used and updated the findings of the borough-wide Equality Impact Assessment completed in 2018" when deciding to locate the service at the Town Hall.


Asked for that update, the council replied: 

> "The Council does not hold recorded information constituting an update to the earlier Equality Analysis Assessment. No separate or discrete updated assessment was created for Southall Town Hall."


It also holds no record of how the earlier assessment was applied to the Town Hall, and no record of a decision, or reasons, not to carry out a Town Hall-specific assessment. Since the service opened, it has done no site-specific equality, safeguarding or community-impact monitoring: 

> "The Council does not undertake location-specific monitoring or evaluation of this nature."


The council says the wording "was intended to refer to the earlier assessment informing subsequent operational consideration; it did not refer to a recorded update". It is reviewing the wording.


## A warning from inside the council


The only pre-opening assessment the council says it holds is an email of 8 January 2025 from a team leader in its Safer Communities team. The officer was writing about a proposed side entrance to the Town Hall for RISE clients.


The officer praised RISE's existing work in Southall as "invaluable". But they were "alarmed" that a new alleyway might be created, warning that it "would likely create a further unregulated space that cannot be policed" and "would leave female access user incredibly vulnerable due to the location and limited visibility". 

Referring to the council's work on male violence against women and girls, they concluded: 

> "I strongly believe this is not appropriate."


They added: "I am not sure whether a planning application has been submitted, or whether the location has been surveyed with options considered."


The council says the email informed the final design of the building. The response does not say what changed as a result.


The council also says officers and RISE spoke with its Community Safety team, the Metropolitan Police, Southall Community Alliance and representatives of the neighbouring Mandir, but that it "does not hold records of most of those discussions".


## "No evidence" — on what basis?


In August, and again in September, the council said: 

> "There is no evidence that the current issues are being caused by the relocation of RISE. What we are seeing reflects a more complex picture, including displacement from enforcement activity elsewhere and existing pressures in the area."


Asked what that statement relied on, the council said it was "based on operational intelligence" from the summer's police and council operation in Southall, which "indicated that the majority of individuals identified as involved in criminal or anti-social behaviour had no identified link to services delivered by RISE".


It has "not identified a discrete report, dataset or formal causal analysis created for the purpose of making that statement". It identified no recorded analysis of whether crime or anti-social behaviour changed after the move, and it acknowledged that the ward-level figures it points to "are not a site-specific before-and-after assessment of the Southall Town Hall location". Nothing in the response addresses "displacement from enforcement activity elsewhere".


## What this does and doesn't show


None of this shows that RISE caused crime, or that consultation never happened. People who need drug and alcohol treatment deserve a service, and the council's own officer called the existing one invaluable.


It does show that several assurances given to Southall residents rest on assessments the council cannot produce.


A further response, on what the council's Community Safety team advised about the move, is due shortly. Southall Stories will publish a fuller investigation once it arrives.
