**Question:** Concerns have been raised for several years by nearby residents that development on the former Southall Gasworks site has released toxic chemicals into the local environment, which scientists at Imperial College London have recently started to examine. As the lead researcher Ian Mudway is looking to extend this research into the other gasworks sites across London, will you consider funding these studies?
[Ref: https://www.theguardian.com/business/2023/apr/27/scientists-to-investig…]

**Answer:** The National Planning Policy Framework requires an assessment of risks associated with contamination and remediation proposals and places the responsibility on the landowner/developer. There are also strong regulatory mechanisms, such as the Environmental Protection Act 1990, Building Regulations and Environmental Permitting Regulations, to control the development of contaminated sites. In 2010 Ealing Council refused planning permission for the development, however, the decision was overturned by the then Mayor of London.

Under the GLA Act, I have no responsibilities or enforcement powers over development on contaminated land or land remediation. In this instance, Ealing Council is the local planning authority with responsibility for overseeing all aspects of regeneration in the borough, and ensuring contaminated land is treated to bring it safely back into use. It also has the responsibility to monitor air quality across the borough and to take reasonable steps to consider any complaint relating to nuisance (smells from industry or trade are classed as a statutory nuisance). This would also be the case for the other gasworks sites across London where the responsible body will be the local council. On this basis it would not be appropriate for me to fund studies of the other gasworks sites.
