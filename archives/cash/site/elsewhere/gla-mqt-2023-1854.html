Greater London Authority record · 2023-05-23

Funding studies into chemicals released by redevelopment — Mayor's Question 2023/1854

Polanski asks the Mayor to fund an extension of Imperial College's (Ian Mudway) Southall study to other gasworks. Sadiq Khan declines, saying he has no powers over contaminated land and that Ealing is responsible; he notes the 2010 mayoral overturn of Ealing's refusal.

Source: https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/questions-mayor/find-an-answer/funding-studies-chemicals-released-redevelopment (live, fetched 2026-11-08). Meeting: MQT 18 May 2023. Question by Zack Polanski AM (City Hall Greens), asked of the Mayor. Answer dated Tuesday 23 May 2023. Category: Planning and Housing. GLA material (Open Government Licence). CASH not named.

Question: Concerns have been raised for several years by nearby residents that development on the former Southall Gasworks site has released toxic chemicals into the local environment, which scientists at Imperial College London have recently started to examine. As the lead researcher Ian Mudway is looking to extend this research into the other gasworks sites across London, will you consider funding these studies? [Ref: https://www.theguardian.com/business/2023/apr/27/scientists-to-investig…]

Answer: The National Planning Policy Framework requires an assessment of risks associated with contamination and remediation proposals and places the responsibility on the landowner/developer. There are also strong regulatory mechanisms, such as the Environmental Protection Act 1990, Building Regulations and Environmental Permitting Regulations, to control the development of contaminated sites. In 2010 Ealing Council refused planning permission for the development, however, the decision was overturned by the then Mayor of London.

Under the GLA Act, I have no responsibilities or enforcement powers over development on contaminated land or land remediation. In this instance, Ealing Council is the local planning authority with responsibility for overseeing all aspects of regeneration in the borough, and ensuring contaminated land is treated to bring it safely back into use. It also has the responsibility to monitor air quality across the borough and to take reasonable steps to consider any complaint relating to nuisance (smells from industry or trade are classed as a statutory nuisance). This would also be the case for the other gasworks sites across London where the responsible body will be the local council. On this basis it would not be appropriate for me to fund studies of the other gasworks sites.

Ealing Council’s website contains detailed information on Southall Gasworks which can be found here.

The GLA has also published a planning practice note on dealing with contaminated and polluted sites which can be accessed here.
