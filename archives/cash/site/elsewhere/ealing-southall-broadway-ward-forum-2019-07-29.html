Southall Broadway Ward Forum 29 July 2019 Air quality in Southall, concerns about health and the odour

In response to residents’ complaints about odours from the Southall Gasworks site the council asked Public Health England to review the data. This has been done and they have published their interim report and findings in ‘Public health England and Southall Waterside interim health risk assessment (review of the air quality monitoring data)’ Version 3 published 9 July 2019.

Public Health England (PHE) looked at air quality data collected since Jan 2018- May 2019 (by Atkins an accredited environmental consultancy). These data are measurements of volatile organic compounds (VOC). The PHE report notes that the most likely source of these chemicals was the remediation work in the soil treatment hospital positioned in the south-western portion of the Southall Gasworks/Waterside site.

The soil hospital closed in March and removed from site in April 2019.

These chemicals are also found in other sources: - Benzene is found in vehicle exhausts, evaporation of petrol and petrol manufacturing - Naphthalene is found in vehicle exhaust, use of solvents or creosote, also used in mothballs and has a very distinctive smell - Trichloroethylene is found in the tarmacking industry, asphalting of roads, bitumen production - 4-isopropyltoluene, also known as ρ-cymene is an ingredient commonly used as a fragrance agent in polishes and sanitation goods such as soaps and detergents, and is likely to be an ingredient in the odour suppressant used on site

The data have been reviewed and compared against health-based air quality guidelines, standards and assessment levels. PHE note these chemicals would be dispersed and diluted by the large volume of air between the monitoring point and the boundary of any home (or person); additional risk to health is expressed in terms of average exposure over a whole lifetime; the average exposure in this case was over 47 weeks. The results indicate there is unlikely to be a direct toxicological risk to the health of the nearby population.

Concentrations have decreased following closure of the soil treatment hospital. There is a relatively small area of the former gasworks site under the control of Cadent (a utilities company) who have applied to do some soil remediation work; it is proposed the soil will be removed off-site for remediation. There will be ongoing monitoring of air quality so that the potential health risk can be minimised and kept under review.

Public concerns about the odour were noted. The human nose is very sensitive to odours and some odorous substances were present at levels that can cause stress and anxiety, and symptoms such as nausea, headaches and dizziness, although these are below the levels where there is likely to be a direct toxicologic effect.

PHE have recommended that measures continue to be taken to reduce off-site odours from the construction site, as it is acknowledged that odours can affect an individual’s wellbeing.

In addition, the Environment Agency have made recommendations for monitoring and managing the odours, and these have all been met by the developer.

Important information If you are being affected by odour use the Council’s Response service to report it on 020 8825 8111 The Environment Agency should also be contacted on their Incident Hotline number 0800 80 70 60 Council officers will aim to visit to witness the nuisance and will liaise as appropriate with the Environment Agency (EA) and the developer. The EA will inform the site operator of any odour reports received, having removed personal information from the reports shared. If an odour is causing concern for health, please consult your GP or NHS 111.
